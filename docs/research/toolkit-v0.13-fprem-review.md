The producer change is correct and suitably minimal for the observed finite-operand CRT loop. `lifter.py:3931-3938` already computes an entire host remainder with `fmod` or `remainder` in one call. Clearing C2 afterwards makes status agree that this modeled operation completed. Previously a finite FXAM published C2=1 (`templates/runtime/recomp_types.h:299-303`) and remainder never updated it; `fnstsw` subsequently exposed the stale status (`lifter.py:4029-4034`). A polling loop could therefore wait forever despite having a fully reduced value.

Intel's primary manual confirms that C2 represents incomplete reduction and that completion also defines quotient bits C0/C3/C1. See [Intel SDM Volume 1, x87 condition codes](https://www.intel.com/content/dam/www/public/us/en/documents/manuals/64-ia-32-architectures-software-developer-vol-1-manual.pdf) and [Intel SDM Volume 2, FPREM/FPREM1](https://cdrdv2-public.intel.com/774492/325383-sdm-vol-2abcd.pdf). This review interprets the patch within the toolkit's existing complete host-remainder model; it does not claim instruction-perfect x87 implementation.

The new compiled tests at `test_x87_classification.py:58` are discriminating. They seed the exact status bit that caused the stop, exercise both remainder variants at all eight TOP positions, pin their differing quotient behavior (8/3 yields 2.0 for truncated quotient and -1.0 for nearest-even quotient), require unchanged stack TOP/divisor, and bound polling to three iterations. The negative control removes only the emitted C2 clear and requires failure. Its finite bounded loop and subprocess timeout cannot hang the fixture indefinitely. Expected remainders are independent explicit values. The first prepared oracle incorrectly expected -1.5 for FPREM1 at 7.5/3; compiled execution caught that mistake. Nearest-even rounds quotient 2.5 to 2, so both operations yield +1.5 for that tie. The bounded fixture was corrected to 8/3; real-x86 cases retain the tie coverage.

The real-x86 cases at `cases.py:349-358` add signed dividend/divisor, zero, integer and tie inputs, all with small exponents so the hardware completes one reduction. Masking `fnstsw ax` down to 0x0400 intentionally tests C2 while avoiding unrelated currently unmodeled quotient bits. The fpu-kind harness independently compares eax, all live stack values and stack depth (`cases.py:11-35`), so a wrong remainder or incorrect pop cannot be hidden by that mask. Loading the divisor first and dividend second correctly leaves dividend in ST(0), divisor in ST(1).

No blocking fixture or producer issue was found.
