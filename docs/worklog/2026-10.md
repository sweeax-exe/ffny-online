- 2026-10-02 04:40 — **The start-up hang: a kernel timer's signal lost on re-arm (patch 0103). The 02:40
  reading (2) was wrong about the clock.** A read of the movie player (`docs/research/movie-audio-clock.md`)
  showed its clock is not the sound stream but a 120 Hz tick counter, `[0x3C9684]`, advanced by a thread
  (`sub_001ED5A0`) that a timeSetEvent-style callback (`sub_001ED620`, re-armed through `sub_001F9A8A`)
  wakes; XAPI's timer thread `sub_001F98A2` drives that with `KeSetTimerEx` on a synchronization timer and
  an infinite `KeWaitForMultipleObjects` on [wake event, timer]. The catcher's next hang (boot 10,
  `run-20261002-024846`) fits: the tick counter stopped at 0x101D (34 s of ticks) in every sample, the tick
  thread waits on its event, and `sub_001F98A2` sits in `KeWaitForMultipleObjects` for good. The GPU and the
  sound streams are bystanders. Cause, in `kernel_set_timer`: the timer was put in the firing table under
  the lock and its shadow event reset *after* the lock was dropped; the runtime's timer thread (time
  critical, same core, 1 ms cadence) can fire a timer that is already due in between, and the reset then
  wipes the signal. The chain re-arms itself only from the callback, so one lost signal stops the clock for
  ever. Now the reset (and SignalState = 0) happens before arming, under the lock. Second race, found by
  reading and fixed in the same patch: `ke_object_resolve` made a lazy shadow event without holding the
  table's lock across look-up and insert, so a waiter and a setter meeting an inline-initialised KEVENT for
  the first time each made one and the second insert closed the handle the waiter slept on. Result: 45 of
  45 boots reach the main menu (`run-20261002-025051` .. `042x`), against 5 hangs in ~74 boots before
  (4 of ~50, 0 of 14, 1 of 10); that is strong, not proof (about 1 in 25 by chance). Goldens M2/M3/M4a
  match; a debug fight holds 116-120 presents per 2 s, no crash (`run-20261002-043246`). 0102:
  `RECOMP_APU_TRACE=<n>` sets how many progress lines the APU prints. Still to check: whether the rare boot
  with no sound was this too (a DirectSound timer on the same path would stall the same way).
